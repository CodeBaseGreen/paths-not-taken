Reading notes (14 pages) and a five-voice panel on Ruben Laukkonen and colleagues' paper *Contemplative Artificial
Intelligence* (2025, [arXiv:2504.15125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15125)), which the
[Better AI]({{ '/posts/better-ai-as-in-better-for-humans/' | relative_url }}) paper cites as its closest parallel.

The paper argues that rules, feedback and filters applied from outside will weaken as AI outgrows its overseers,
and that alignment should instead be built into an AI's **world model**. It draws four principles from
contemplative traditions:

- **Mindfulness:** watching one's own processes and sub-goals, without a stake in what is found.
- **Emptiness:** holding every goal and belief as provisional, a representation rather than a fact.
- **Non-duality:** not treating self and other as fundamentally separate.
- **Boundless care:** an orientation toward reducing everyone's suffering, guarding against "cold wisdom" and
  "dumb compassion".

Each is expressed in active inference as a precision weight. The paper proposes three ways to build them in:
architecture, a contemplative constitution, and reinforcement on the chain of thought. In two pilot experiments,
simply prompting language models with the principles improved safety on the AILuminate benchmark and sharply
raised cooperation in the iterated Prisoner's Dilemma.

The notes follow the argument section by section. Where the paper draws on Mahayana sources, boxes give the
nearest early-discourse parallels, such as MN 121 on emptiness, MN 22's raft, and SN 47.19's acrobats. Part II sets
out the overlaps and differences with this blog's Phase 2. The biggest difference is in how a concept is held
lightly: they *lower the confidence* placed in it, while Phase 2 *looks beneath it* and tests it by replay.
