The companion to [Better AI, as in Better for Humans]({{ '/posts/better-ai-as-in-better-for-humans/' | relative_url }})
(10 pages), with a five-voice panel. It's a first version, freer than the main paper, and uses the Pali terms and the
specific discourses.

Each teaching comes first in its own terms, then a network reading:

- **Paṭicca-samuppāda**, link by link, from avijjā to jarāmaraṇa. "With the cessation of this, that ceases" is the
  knockout test.
- **The khandhas and anattā.** The five aggregates as a layer diagram, and MN 35's not-self argument as an
  intervention test: can the self-variable set the layers at will?
- **Vedanā, the anusaya and the second arrow** (SN 36.6, MN 44), with the second arrow measured by replay.
- **Papañca** (MN 18): proliferation, the mind spreading out by association. Large language models are, in this
  precise sense, proliferation engines. Its remedy is **yoniso manasikāra** (MN 2), attention to the source.
- **Cetanā and the mind's inclination** (AN 6.63, MN 19): "whatever one frequently thinks becomes the inclination
  of the mind" read as a statement about training.
- **Satipaṭṭhāna** (MN 10), inside and outside: the same attention turned on oneself and others.
- **The nīvaraṇa and bojjhaṅga**, and MN 20's five methods as five kinds of intervention, with suppression last.
- **The brahmavihāras, right speech and Insight Dialogue**: MN 21's simile of the saw, and MN 58's child with a
  pebble as a rebuke to engagement optimisation.
- **The art**: the LED creatures as a testbed free to be wrong, and pet-like creatures as one deliberate,
  relatable form of human interaction with better AI, not assumed to be the best.
