The main reference paper for the second phase of this research (12 pages), with a five-voice panel discussion.

Phase 1 argued for learners built around counterfactuals. They replay themselves with one change, hold their
structure as inspectable graphs, and test their concepts by intervention, rather than relying on very large
associative networks. This paper says what that was for. A learner built that way has **separate layers** for
sensing, valuing, urging, acting and observing itself, and so it can do something a monolithic network can't: look
at the processes beneath its own concepts.

The driving analogy comes from the insight tradition of early Buddhism:

> **Insight is awareness of the level of abstraction beneath the one in use.**

The paper takes each teaching in its own terms first, and only then asks what it means for a rung-3 agent:

- **What "better" means**, taken from the discourses rather than invented: the three harmful roots (greed,
  aversion, delusion) and their opposites, the four boundless qualities, and the five marks of well-spoken words.
- **Dependent origination as an agent loop**, with the traditional gap between feeling tone and craving becoming an
  architectural feature: a place where valuation is observed rather than obeyed.
- **The five hindrances and seven factors of awakening**, with what feeds and starves each (SN 46.51). Wise
  attention, attention "to the source", turns out to be causal rather than associative attention. The balance
  teaching (SN 46.53) reads as a control law on arousal.
- **Why insight leads toward compassion**, stated as a testable hypothesis about agents that model others with the
  same machinery they use for themselves.
- **AI as a layer between people.** Following Gregory Kramer's Insight Dialogue: the dharma is relational, and
  relationships now run through a designed technological layer. Kramer's six guidelines become design qualities.
- **Thirteen research threads**, light parallels with Goertzel, Laukkonen et al. and Doctor & Levin, and honest
  limits.

The vocabulary ("craving", "clinging") is explanatory: a concept map for patterns of behaviour, with no claim about
machine experience. The references include a section of primary sources from the Middle Length Discourses,
cited like science.
