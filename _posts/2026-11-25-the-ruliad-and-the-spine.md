A short introduction (4 pages), with a panel discussion. It reads three bodies of work only for what connects to
this blog's argument. It isn't a standalone introduction to any of them, and their physics claims, which are
contested, are not endorsed here.

- **Stephen Wolfram's ruliad** supplies a vocabulary that fits replayable worlds well:
  - **Multiway histories:** a seeded world is a small branching space its owner explores one paired branch at a time.
  - **Distance between branches:** a continuous measure for fork maps.
  - **Causal invariance:** the reason for the blog's determinism rules.
  - **Bounded observers:** coarse concepts, with people as coarse judges by design.
  - **Pockets of reducibility:** the only places curiosity and coarse causal graphs can work.
  - **Rulial space:** breeding and library learning as changes in the language rules are written in.
- **Constructor theory** (Chiara Marletto and David Deutsch) states laws as what *can and can't* be done. That is
  the cleanest form of the blog's irreversibility thesis. Marletto's replicator-and-vehicle argument also maps
  onto copies versus lineages, and onto the creatures' genes and bodies.
- **Hector Zenil's algorithmic information dynamics** scores interventions by how they change an object's
  compressed description. It's a computable cousin of the counterfactual lab's knock-out attribution.

A light sceptical note from Philip Dawid marks where counterfactual talk needs care. He's right at the live
boundary, and side-stepped inside a replayable world.
